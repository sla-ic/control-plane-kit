# Sommelier and perfumery: expert sensory triage traditions
> Mined via open-learning re-run (Sonnet).

## Structured sensory grid (WSET systematic approach)
**Term of art:** Systematic Approach to Tasting (SAT)
A sommelier does not free-associate when evaluating a wine. They work through a mandatory sequence — appearance, nose (intensity, development, specific aromas), palate (sweetness, acidity, tannin, alcohol, body, flavor intensity, finish) — before forming any conclusion. Each dimension has a fixed vocabulary of allowed descriptors drawn from a controlled list. The grid ensures that perception is complete before interpretation begins, and that nothing gets skipped because the evaluator's attention was grabbed early by a dominant signal. The artifact is the tasting grid itself, which functions as an attention allocation scaffold: it forces the human to visit every channel of the sensory space before collapsing to a judgment.
**Failure mode:** Halo capture — a single vivid note (Brett, volatile acidity, excessive oak) overwhelms the grid traversal, and the evaluator shortcuts to verdict. The grid is the guard against premature closure.

## Retro-olfaction and finish timing
**Term of art:** Retronasal length / finish (measured in seconds)
Sommeliers distinguish between the aromas detected orthonasally (sniffing the glass) and retronasal aromas that develop after the wine is swallowed or expectorated. They measure finish length — how long flavor persists — in precise seconds, and use this duration as an independent quality signal separate from initial impact. The mechanism is temporal gating: the expert divides the sensory stream into phases (attack, mid-palate, finish) and treats each phase as carrying distinct information. What appears only late in the sequence often carries the most diagnostic weight about structure and aging potential.
**Failure mode:** Recency suppression — the most intense initial impression (high alcohol heat, residual sugar) is disproportionately weighted, and the evaluator stops attending before the finish develops. Finish timing disciplines the human to hold attention through the full temporal arc.

## Blind tasting deduction cascade
**Term of art:** Blind deduction (flying blind / service deduction)
Sommeliers are trained to withhold label identity entirely and work backward from sensory evidence to origin, grape, and vintage. The practice forces the expert to build an internal model of what a signal pattern means before confirming with external metadata. The deduction proceeds in constraint order — climate clues (acidity, alcohol) narrow to region; color and structure narrow to variety; development level narrows to vintage range — rather than following personal preference. The artifact is the deduction ladder: a hierarchical elimination tree that converts sensory input into identity claims without the anchor of prior knowledge.
**Failure mode:** Confirmation anchoring — if the label is visible, the expert's perception bends toward expected characteristics. Blind protocol guards against the label overriding what the wine actually presents.

## Olfactory fatigue management / palate cleansing
**Term of art:** Olfactory adaptation / nose reset
Both sommeliers and perfumers know that the olfactory system adapts to a constant stimulus within seconds, rendering it invisible. Sommeliers manage this by working through flights in a fixed order (light to heavy, dry to sweet), inserting neutral intervals (water, plain bread), and sniffing their own skin or wool sleeve to reset. Master perfumers use coffee beans as a palate-cleansing counterscent — or simply rest in fresh air — between evaluations. The practice treats the sensing organ itself as a resource that depletes and must be actively managed between queries to the sensory environment.
**Failure mode:** Fatigue blindness — the evaluator runs consecutive heavy samples without reset intervals, loses sensitivity for a specific compound class (e.g., sulfur, toast), and stops detecting it entirely. Nose reset protocols guard against evaluator exhaustion masquerading as absence of signal.

## Perfumer's brief and olfactory translation
**Term of art:** The brief (also: olfactory brief / creative brief)
In commercial perfumery, a client's abstract intent — "convey success without aggression," "evoke a 1960s Roman holiday" — must be translated into a precise formula of raw materials. The perfumeur translates non-olfactory language (emotional, visual, narrative) into chemical ingredient decisions through an intermediate artifact called the brief. The brief captures both the client's expressive intent and the technical constraints (budget, allergen limits, base materials), and serves as the shared reference that prevents misalignment between what was requested and what was constructed. Multiple rounds of trial flacons are then evaluated against the brief, not against the perfumer's personal taste.
**Failure mode:** Brief drift — without the written artifact, the perfumer defaults to their own aesthetic preferences or latest trend exposure, progressively decoupling from the original intent. The brief functions as the external memory that holds the goal stable across iterations.

## Accords and the irreducibility of the blend
**Term of art:** Accord (also: chord)
A perfumery accord is a combination of two or more raw materials that, when blended, produces a unified olfactory impression that does not smell like any of its components individually. Perfumers learn to think in accords rather than single ingredients — the evaluation unit is the emergent whole, not the constituent parts. When deconstructing a competitor fragrance or troubleshooting a formula, the expert works to identify the accord structure (what combination produces which impression) rather than listing individual molecules. This shapes how they attend to complex signals: they listen for gestalt patterns first, then disaggregate.
**Failure mode:** Reductionism — evaluating ingredients in isolation misses emergent interactions (synergies, suppressions), so a formula that passes individual-ingredient review still fails as a blend. Training in accords guards against component-level adequacy masking system-level failure.

## Vintage and provenance narrative (context-loaded evaluation)
**Term of art:** Terroir narrative / vintage condition memory
Expert sommeliers store detailed vintage condition records (frost events, harvest timing, disease pressure) for major regions across decades. When evaluating a wine, they actively retrieve and overlay this context — not as bias, but as a prior that helps them interpret ambiguous signals. A characteristic that would be a flaw in a warm year becomes appropriate expression in a difficult cool year. The mechanism is conditional interpretation: the same signal has different meaning depending on the context frame. The sommelier carries this contextual library in memory and uses it to convert ambiguous sensory data into calibrated assessments.
**Failure mode:** Context-free judgment — evaluating against an absolute standard rather than the constraints of the vintage produces false positives (penalizing structurally correct wines for their vintage character) and false negatives (excusing actual flaws as "vintage variation"). Provenance memory is the guard against acontextual verdicts.

## Service sequencing and the degustation architecture
**Term of art:** Degustation menu / flight sequencing
A sommelier pairing a multi-course meal constructs a sequence where each wine prepares the palate for the next. The logic is cumulative: residual acidity from a white cleans fat from a previous course; a lighter red before a heavy one ensures the heavy one reads as structured rather than merely big. The sequence is designed not for any individual moment but for how the cumulative sensory state of the diner evolves through the meal. The artifact is the pairing matrix — a planned arc of states that the sommelier engineers in advance. The human (the diner) is navigated through a sequence that would be impossible to design moment-by-moment.
**Failure mode:** Inversion fatigue — serving in wrong order (heavy to light, tannic to delicate) permanently impairs perception of subsequent wines; the diner's sensory state is path-dependent and cannot be reset mid-service. Sequencing guards against irreversible degradation of the evaluation environment.

## What this field knows
Sommelier and perfumery traditions understand that expert perception is not passive reception but an active, managed process subject to depletion, anchoring, sequencing effects, and halo capture — and that the interface between the expert and the sensory environment must be architected, not left to real-time improvisation. Both fields developed structured artifacts (the tasting grid, the brief, the accord vocabulary, the flight sequence) specifically to prevent the most vivid or earliest signal from monopolizing attention at the cost of weaker but equally diagnostic signals. They also discovered that the sensing instrument itself (the human nose and palate) is a stateful resource that degrades without active management, and that the temporal arc of a signal — what appears late, what lingers, what emerges only in context — often carries more meaning than the initial impression.

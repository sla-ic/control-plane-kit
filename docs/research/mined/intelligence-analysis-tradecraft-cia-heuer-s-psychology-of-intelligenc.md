## Indicators & Warning (I&W) lists — predefined observable signposts, written BEFORE the crisis, that an apparatus watches continuously; only their tripping (a status CHANGE), not raw activity, escalates to the human.

**Term of art:** Indications & Warning (I&W); watch list; warning problem; the 'concept' (ha-Konseptsia)

Analysts pre-commit, in calm conditions, to a concrete list of observables that would have to be true for a given scenario to be unfolding (e.g., for an invasion: reservist call-ups, blood-bank stockpiling, field-hospital deployment, comms blackout, leadership families evacuating). The collection apparatus then monitors against this fixed scaffold. The human is NOT shown the activity stream; they are shown which indicators have flipped and the resulting movement of the scenario's likelihood. Crucially, the list is authored ahead of time precisely so that judgment under stress doesn't have to invent the relevance criteria in the moment — the hard cognitive work of 'what would matter' is front-loaded into a quiet period.

**Failure mode:** Indicators become stale or are gamed by an adaptive adversary (deception); a 'cry wolf' problem when indicators trip for benign reasons erodes the principal's willingness to act; and the catastrophic failure mode — the surprise comes via a path NOT on the list (the unlisted indicator), so the very instrument that focuses attention also blinds it to the novel. Yom Kippur 1973: indicators were tripping but were explained away under a fixed 'concept' (ha-Konseptsia) that Egypt wouldn't attack without certain preconditions.

## Analysis of Competing Hypotheses (ACH) — Heuer's signature technique: an explicit matrix of all plausible hypotheses scored against each piece of evidence by DIAGNOSTICITY, structured to find what DISCONFIRMS rather than what confirms.

**Term of art:** Analysis of Competing Hypotheses (ACH); diagnosticity; disconfirming evidence; the most-consistent vs. least-inconsistent distinction

The analyst lays every serious hypothesis across the columns and every piece of evidence down the rows, then for each cell asks not 'is this consistent with the hypothesis I favor?' but 'how much does this evidence DISCRIMINATE between hypotheses?' Evidence consistent with every hypothesis (high availability, low diagnosticity) is explicitly discarded as worthless for decision — a radical inversion, because the most salient/vivid evidence is often the least diagnostic. You then seek to REFUTE hypotheses: the surviving hypothesis is the one with the least disconfirming evidence, not the most supporting. The matrix itself is the interface artifact — it externalizes reasoning so a reviewer can audit WHY a conclusion rose, and so a single damning data point can topple a comfortable consensus.

**Failure mode:** Garbage-in if the hypothesis set is incomplete (the true explanation was never a column); deception specifically exploits diagnosticity by feeding evidence engineered to be consistent with the wrong hypothesis; analysts backslide into using the matrix to justify a pre-held view (confirmation bias reasserts itself inside the tool). It is also labor-intensive, so it is reserved for high-stakes calls, not routine flow.

## The 'so what?' / Bottom Line Up Front (BLUF) discipline and the inverted-pyramid finished-intelligence form — the judgment and its decision-relevance come FIRST, with evidence and caveats accessible but subordinated.

**Term of art:** BLUF (Bottom Line Up Front); 'so what'; Key Judgments; President's Daily Brief (PDB); finished vs. raw intelligence

Finished intelligence (the President's Daily Brief, a National Intelligence Estimate's Key Judgments) leads with the assessed conclusion and explicitly states why the consumer should care RIGHT NOW — the 'so what' — before any supporting detail. The form is engineered to the consumer's attention budget: a principal who reads only the first sentence still gets the decision-relevant judgment; depth is layered beneath for those who drill. This is a deliberate progressive-disclosure architecture: the apparatus has done the perceiving and the weighing, and what rises is a compressed JUDGMENT plus its action-relevance, not a digest of inputs. The PDB historically was tailored per-principal, even hand-carried and discussed, because what 'rises' is a function of the specific reader's portfolio and pending decisions.

**Failure mode:** Compression can launder away the uncertainty that mattered (the famous '16 words' / Iraq aluminum tubes: dissent and caveats existed in the body but the headline judgment carried false confidence); BLUF rewards a confident-sounding line, pressuring analysts toward unwarranted certainty; tailoring to the consumer shades into telling the principal what they want to hear (politicization / 'intelligence to please').

## Calibrated estimative language and Words of Estimative Probability (WEP) — a standardized, shared vocabulary that makes the human-system trust seam legible by separating likelihood, confidence, and source quality at the point of consumption.

**Term of art:** Words of Estimative Probability (WEP); Sherman Kent's spectrum; ICD 203; likelihood vs. confidence (two-axis); Admiralty Code / source-reliability matrix (A-F / 1-6)

Sherman Kent's insight: words like 'possible' or 'likely' silently meant wildly different probabilities to different readers, so the field built a controlled lexicon mapping verbal terms to probability bands ('almost certainly' ~ 90-99%, 'probably/likely' ~ 55-80%, 'roughly even chance', 'unlikely', 'remote'). ICD 203 now MANDATES that estimative judgments use this calibrated language AND separately express analytic CONFIDENCE (high/moderate/low) — which is a function of evidence quality and source reliability — as a DISTINCT axis from likelihood. So the interface forces a high-likelihood/low-confidence judgment to look different from a high-likelihood/high-confidence one. Source reliability is itself tagged (the Admiralty/NATO scale: source reliability A-F crossed with information credibility 1-6). The point: the human must act on what they can't verify, so the apparatus owes them a precise, standardized signal of how much to lean on each claim.

**Failure mode:** False precision — attaching numbers to vibes; readers still collapse confidence and likelihood despite the two-axis design; 'moderate confidence' becomes a hedge that protects the analyst rather than informs the consumer; and the lexicon can create an illusion of rigor that survives even when the underlying sourcing is thin (a single deceptive source, e.g., 'Curveball,' propagated as if corroborated).

## Key Assumptions Check (KAC) and 'linchpin analysis' — explicitly surfacing the small number of load-bearing assumptions on which a judgment pivots, so the apparatus can monitor THOSE for change rather than everything.

**Term of art:** Key Assumptions Check (KAC); linchpin analysis (MacEachin); mirror-imaging; Jeremiah Report

Before locking an assessment, analysts enumerate the assumptions that, if wrong, would collapse the conclusion — the 'linchpins' (Doug MacEachin's term). Each assumption is stress-tested: How confident are we? What would have to happen for it to be false? Could an adversary make it false? This converts a monolithic judgment into a few named dependencies. The transfer to the interface is precise: instead of re-perceiving the whole world, the system watches the linchpins and raises an alert specifically when an assumption-violating signal appears. It also creates a tripwire for self-deception — the 1998 Indian nuclear test surprise (Jeremiah post-mortem) was blamed on the unexamined mirror-imaging assumption that a new government wouldn't follow through on campaign promises; a KAC would have flagged that assumption as load-bearing and untested.

**Failure mode:** The most dangerous assumptions are the invisible ones — those so deeply held they aren't recognized AS assumptions (mirror-imaging, 'they think like us'); teams list comfortable assumptions and miss the foundational one; and once written, an assumptions list can ossify into doctrine that is defended rather than re-examined.

## Institutionalized contrarian/red-team channels — devil's advocacy, Team A/Team B, the 'Tenth Man' / 'dissent channel,' and Red Cell — structures that GUARANTEE a challenge to the rising consensus reaches the human alongside it.

**Term of art:** Devil's advocacy; Team A/Team B; Ipcha Mistabra ('the Tenth Man'); dissent channel; Red Cell; competitive analysis

Because consensus-seeking and premature closure are predictable, the field builds dissent into the pipeline as a STANDING role, not an optional mood. Forms include: a devil's advocate assigned to attack the lead judgment; Team A/Team B competitive analysis (two teams work the same data to opposed conclusions, both surfaced to the principal); the Israeli post-Yom-Kippur 'Tenth Man' (Ipcha Mistabra) — a mandated role obligated to argue the opposite if everyone agrees; the State Department dissent channel; and the CIA Red Cell (stood up after 9/11) tasked with deliberately unconventional, 'what are we missing' provocations. The key design property: the contrarian view is delivered TO the decision-maker, not filtered out by the same hierarchy that produced the mainline view — so what rises includes a structured minority report.

**Failure mode:** Ritualized devil's advocacy that everyone discounts ('that's just the red team's job'); the contrarian gets captured by the group over time; Team B can be stacked to produce a politically desired alarmist answer (the 1976 Team B on Soviet capability is the cautionary case — competitive analysis weaponized); and dissent that is technically 'on record' in a footnote but never actually rises to the principal's attention (footnote wars in NIEs).

## The signal-vs-noise / 'noise' doctrine (Wohlstetter) — the foundational recognition that after the fact every signal is obvious, but BEFORE the fact the relevant signal is buried in overwhelming, contradictory, plausibly-benign noise, and the design problem is detection under that ratio.

**Term of art:** Signal vs. noise; Wohlstetter's Pearl Harbor; 'connect the dots' fallacy; pattern-of-life / baseline; denial & deception (D&D)

Roberta Wohlstetter's Pearl Harbor study established the field's core epistemics: the warning signs were all there in the record, but so were countless contradictory signals, and the true signal was indistinguishable from noise in real time without hindsight. This reframes the interface problem away from 'collect the signal' (it was already collected) toward 'how does a true anomaly become distinguishable from benign anomalies fast enough to act.' It drives practices like baseline/pattern-of-life modeling (you can only spot a meaningful deviation against a well-characterized 'normal'), and anomaly-driven (not volume-driven) escalation: the apparatus suppresses the expected and raises the deviation. It also explains the field's humility — it designs assuming the next surprise's signal is, right now, sitting indistinguishable in the noise.

**Failure mode:** Hindsight bias contaminates every post-mortem (the 'connect the dots' fallacy — dots are only obviously connectable afterward), leading to over-correction and alert flooding; raising every anomaly recreates the noise problem one level up (alert fatigue, the 9/11 'system was blinking red' but saturated); and a sophisticated adversary deliberately manufactures benign-looking noise to lower the signal ratio (denial & deception).

## The collection-to-judgment pipeline as named, separated stages (the Intelligence Cycle) with a deliberate PROCESSING/EXPLOITATION filter between raw collection and the analyst — most of what is perceived is designed NEVER to reach a human.

**Term of art:** Intelligence Cycle; Processing & Exploitation; TCPED; Priority Intelligence Requirements (PIRs); requirements/tasking; stovepiping

The doctrinal cycle — Direction → Collection → Processing & Exploitation → Analysis & Production → Dissemination — encodes that raw take (intercepts, imagery, reports) vastly exceeds human capacity, so an explicit triage/processing layer (TCPED in the imagery world: Tasking, Collection, Processing, Exploitation, Dissemination) reduces, translates, correlates, and SUPPRESSES before analysis. 'Requirements' and tasking flow the other direction: the human's standing questions (the consumer's priorities, formalized as PIRs — Priority Intelligence Requirements) actively shape what the apparatus bothers to surface. So the interface is bidirectional: the human's declared decision-needs prune the perception space upstream, and only exploited, correlated material rises. The PIR is the canonical artifact: a small, ranked set of questions tied to the decision-maker's actual choices, against which all collection and surfacing is prioritized.

**Failure mode:** The processing layer becomes the bottleneck and the censor — material that didn't match standing requirements gets dropped (the pre-9/11 mountains of untranslated intercepts); 'requirements' ossify so the apparatus only looks where it was told to look (collection bias, the streetlight effect); and stovepiping — each stage/agency hoards, so correlation that would have made a signal rise never happens (the pre-9/11 'failure to share' / wall between agencies).

## Tearline reporting and 'write-to-release' — engineering a single report at multiple disclosure levels so the right amount rises to each consumer, with sourcing protected above the line and actionable substance below it.

**Term of art:** Tearline; write-to-release; sanitization; need-to-know vs. need-to-share; classification/handling caveats

A tearline is a physical/structural break in a report: above the line sits the sensitive sourcing and full classification; below the line is a sanitized version carrying the actionable judgment, written so it can be 'torn off' and passed to a consumer who lacks clearance for the sources. This solves a precise version of the surfacing problem: the human (or a partner, or a field commander) needs the SO-WHAT and enough to act, but not the means by which it was known. 'Write-to-release' pushes this upstream — analysts compose from the start at the lowest defensible classification so the judgment can actually flow to whoever must act, rather than being trapped by its own sourcing. It embodies a key principle: what rises is calibrated to the consumer's need-to-act and need-to-know, and the same underlying perception is rendered at different resolutions for different humans.

**Failure mode:** Over-sanitization strips the context the consumer needs to calibrate trust (they get a bare assertion with no sense of how solid it is); the tension between 'protect the source' and 'enable the action' can delay or block warning that needed to move fast; and aggregation across many tearlines can inadvertently reveal the very source they were meant to protect.

## Structured 'what would change our mind' tripwires and update protocols — analysts pre-register, at the moment of judgment, the specific future observations that would force a revision, converting a static assessment into a living, monitorable commitment.

**Term of art:** 'What would change my mind'; falsifiers/tripwires; Bayesian updating; premature closure; superforecasting (Tetlock/Good Judgment Project); calibration

Rather than issue a judgment and move on, good tradecraft (and forecasting discipline, e.g., the IARPA/Tetlock 'superforecaster' findings now influential in the Acme) specifies in advance the evidence that would shift the estimate and in which direction — a precommitment that defeats hindsight rationalization and creates explicit monitoring targets for the apparatus. This pairs with disciplined belief-updating: small, frequent revisions as indicators move, rather than rare, violent reversals. The interface implication is sharp: the system isn't just watching the world, it's watching the SPECIFIC pre-named falsifiers of the human's current beliefs, and it raises one when it appears — making 'you should reconsider' a first-class, triggerable event rather than something the human has to remember to do. It also supports the 'update vs. reaffirm' rhythm: each cycle the apparatus prompts 'has anything crossed a tripwire?' so judgments don't silently rot.

**Failure mode:** Analysts set tripwires too high or too vague so they never trigger (protecting the prior); 'paralysis by reassessment' / overreacting to noise if tripwires are too sensitive; and the deepest one — failing to register the falsifier that actually matters because it lies outside the imagined space of futures (the unimagined, not merely the unmonitored). Premature closure remains the chronic enemy: once a judgment is socialized and briefed up the chain, the institutional cost of reversing it suppresses the very updating the tripwire was meant to force.

## What this field knows
This field is the canonical discipline for a single human judgment-maker fed by a collection apparatus that perceives vastly more than they can. Its hard-won, distinctive knowledge — built over decades of strategic surprises, post-mortems, and reform — is NOT "how to gather more" but "how to ration scarce human attention against a flood, and protect human JUDGMENT from its own predictable failures." A few things it knows that other fields don't:

(1) Surprise is almost never a collection failure — it's an attention/interpretation failure. After Pearl Harbor (Wohlstetter), 9/11, Yom Kippur, the Iraq WMD estimate, the recurring lesson is that the signal was present in the noise but didn't RISE, or rose and was rationalized away. So the field obsesses over the interface between "what was perceived" and "what reached judgment," not the perception itself. This is the exact gap a human-system interface must close.

(2) The scarce resource is not information, it's the analyst's reconciled attention — and MORE information past a point does NOT improve judgment, it only inflates CONFIDENCE (Heuer's experiment with horse-racing handicappers). This is a load-bearing, counterintuitive finding: an interface that surfaces more to feel thorough actively harms the human. The field's instruments are therefore mostly about SUBTRACTION and FRAMING, not addition.

(3) Judgment fails in predictable, structured ways (confirmation bias, anchoring, mirror-imaging, premature closure, vividness bias, the tendency to see centralized/coherent plans where there is chaos). Because the failures are predictable, they can be ENGINEERED AGAINST with explicit procedure (Structured Analytic Techniques, ACH, devil's advocacy, Key Assumptions Check). The interface is not neutral plumbing — it is a cognitive prosthesis that must actively counter the human's biases.

(4) The unit of value is the DECISION, not the report. The field formalizes this as "intelligence-to-action": warning exists to drive a decision-maker to do something while there is still time. An indicator only matters if it would CHANGE a decision; warning that arrives after the decision window, or that the principal can't act on, is failure. So "what rises" is defined relative to a specific consumer's specific pending decisions and their action timeline — not by intrinsic importance.

(5) Trust between the human and the apparatus is itself an engineered, fragile artifact — sourcing, confidence language, "what would change our mind" — because the human must act on what they cannot personally verify. The field has explicit doctrine (ICD 203 analytic standards) mandating that uncertainty, source reliability, and assumptions be made legible at the moment of consumption, and separating what is KNOWN from what is ASSESSED from what is the analyst's reasoning. This is the trust protocol a perceive-more system owes its human.

The deepest transfer: this field treats the human-system seam as the primary site of failure and the primary object of design — and it has a century of named techniques, artifacts, and post-mortem-derived failure modes for managing exactly that seam under time pressure, adversarial conditions, and information overload.

## The Indicator List (and its hierarchy: indications -> indicators -> Essential Elements of Information -> Specific Information Requirements -> Named Areas of Interest). Analysts pre-author a finite, standing list of *specific observable changes* that would signify an adversary is moving toward a feared course of action — e.g., 'reservists recalled,' 'field hospitals activated,' 'live ammunition issued to forward units,' 'leadership relocated to wartime command posts.' Each indicator is something a collector can actually observe, tied upward to a threat scenario and downward to a concrete collection task. The list is the contract for what counts as significant; the flood of raw reporting is scored against it.

**Term of art:** Indicator List / Watch List; Essential Elements of Information (EEI); Named Area of Interest (NAI); the 'conception' (ha-Konseptzia) failure

Warning is decided BEFORE the data arrives, not in reaction to it. By committing in advance to 'these specific changes mean something,' the system converts an unbounded interpretive problem ('is anything important happening?') into a bounded matching problem ('which of our pre-named indicators just lit up?'). The list is collaboratively built and periodically re-validated, so it encodes institutional judgment about meaning that no single overwhelmed human has to reconstruct in the moment.

**Failure mode:** Indicator lists ossify and fight the last war — they capture the *previous* surprise's signature and miss novel paths (the adversary does the one thing not on the list). They also breed 'checklist warning': if no listed indicator trips, analysts feel licensed to report calm even when the gestalt is alarming. Yom Kippur 1973 is the canonical case — Egyptian/Syrian preparations were partly visible but were explained away because they matched a 'conception' (AMAN's ha-Konseptzia) that said Egypt would not attack without long-range air capability, so the indicators were re-read as exercises.

## Warning Problems with explicit Warning Thresholds and a tripwire grammar. Each warning problem (e.g., 'North Korean attack on the South') is decomposed so that crossing a defined threshold of accumulated indicators triggers a mandatory reporting action. The threshold is a pre-negotiated line, not an analyst's daily mood — when X of these conditions are observed, a warning MUST be issued regardless of whether the analyst personally believes war is coming.

**Term of art:** Warning Problem; Warning Threshold; tripwire; salami-slicing / gradualism; alert fatigue / 'cry wolf'

Thresholds remove the human's moment-to-moment discretion about whether to escalate, which is exactly the discretion that habituation and normalcy bias corrupt. By binding the alert to an objective crossing, the system fires even when the on-watch human's intuition has been lulled. It is a deliberate Ulysses-pact: the alert designers don't trust their future, fatigued, pattern-numbed selves, so they pre-wire the trigger.

**Failure mode:** Threshold-setting is brutally hard: set too low, you cry wolf and the decision-maker tunes you out (alert fatigue, the boy-who-cried-wolf dynamic that desensitized U.S. commanders before Pearl Harbor after repeated war-warnings); set too high, you miss the event. Adversaries also exploit known thresholds by staying just under them (salami-slicing / gradualism) so no single tripwire ever fires while the aggregate situation deteriorates — the 'boiling frog' that defeats discrete tripwires.

## Separation of WARNING from DECISION, and redefining the cost function: a warning that triggers precaution later deemed 'unnecessary' is counted a success, not a false alarm. Warning doctrine (e.g., Cynthia Grabo's 'Anticipating Surprise,' the bible of the field) insists the warner's product is *decision time and freedom from surprise* for the policymaker — not a prediction that will be graded right/wrong. The warner explicitly does NOT own the response; the decision-maker does.

**Term of art:** Grabo's 'Anticipating Surprise'; warning vs. estimate distinction; 'failure of warning' vs. 'failure to act'; the actionability problem

This re-frames the whole attention economy. Normal alerting systems are punished for false positives, so they raise thresholds and go quiet. I&W deliberately tolerates 'false' alarms because the asymmetry of being surprised is catastrophic and irreversible, while a precaution taken in vain is cheap and reversible. By divorcing 'I am warning you' from 'I am telling you what to do,' it lets the system push something to the human's attention without usurping the human's judgment — the human still closes the loop by choosing the response.

**Failure mode:** Warners who fear being blamed for 'wrong' warnings drift toward hedged, ass-covering language ('possibly,' 'cannot be ruled out') that conveys no actionable signal — the 'warning fatigue' and 'analyst hedging' problem. Conversely, if warners are never held accountable at all, warning inflates until it's worthless. And policymakers frequently receive a clear warning and still do nothing (warning was given before Pearl Harbor, Yom Kippur, and 9/11) — the loop fails on the *human's* side, not the system's, revealing that delivering signal is necessary but not sufficient for joint action.

## Graduated readiness states and pre-scripted, jointly-rehearsed response ladders (DEFCON / alert conditions / Watch Conditions). The output of warning is mapped to a small set of named, escalating posture levels, each with pre-defined actions both the system and humans take at that level. The level itself is the message — a single token that compresses a vast situational assessment into one shared, unambiguous state.

**Term of art:** DEFCON / LERTCON / Watch Condition (WATCHCON); readiness state; escalation ladder; inadvertent escalation

Instead of pushing a narrative the human must parse under stress, the interface pushes a *state change* (DEFCON 4 -> 3) that everyone has pre-agreed maps to specific joint actions. The cognitive load at the moment of crisis is minimized because the meaning-and-action binding was done in calm conditions and drilled. It turns 'what rises to attention' into 'which rung are we on,' and 'for what joint action' is already answered by the rung's playbook.

**Failure mode:** Coarse levels lose nuance and can over- or under-react; level changes are politically/diplomatically loaded (raising alert posture can itself provoke the adversary — the security-dilemma / inadvertent-escalation trap). Rigid ladders also struggle with ambiguous warning where you're unsure which rung you're truly on, and there's a documented reluctance to escalate posture for fear of 'looking alarmist' or incurring the cost/disruption of a higher readiness state.

## The 24/7 Watch / Watch Officer / Indications Center, and the formal watch turnover (pass-down log / watch brief). A continuously-staffed human node owns the live picture; on shift change, the outgoing watch hands the incoming watch an explicit, structured pass-down of what's active, what's anomalous, and what to keep eyes on. There is always a single named human accountable for 'the watch' at any instant.

**Term of art:** Watch Officer / Senior Watch Officer; Indications Center / Watch Floor; pass-down log / turnover brief; chain of custody of the picture; normalization of deviance

Continuity of attention is engineered against the reality that no human can attend continuously. The watch construct guarantees an unbroken chain of custody over the warning picture and a single throat to choke — the alert is always OWNED. The turnover ritual is a forcing function that re-surfaces standing concerns so they don't fall through the cracks between humans, and it carries forward the 'so what / watch for this next' framing rather than raw status.

**Failure mode:** Pass-down decay: anomalies get dropped or flattened across handoffs ('the night watch saw something odd' that never propagates), the long-running slow-burn indicator gets normalized over many shifts until no one flags it (normalization of deviance, à la the Challenger O-rings), and watch-standers under flood triage by recency/volume rather than significance. Aviation and nuclear-ops sister fields formalized handoff checklists precisely because informal turnovers lose the critical weak signal.

## Anomaly-and-baseline tradecraft: 'pattern of life,' 'establish normal to detect abnormal,' and 'absence of expected activity is itself an indicator.' Warning analysts invest heavily in characterizing the adversary's NORMAL rhythm so that deviation — including the dog that didn't bark (expected traffic going silent, routine exercises NOT happening, a market suddenly emptying) — becomes the signal that rises.

**Term of art:** Pattern of life; baseline / 'normalcy'; negative indicator / 'the dog that didn't bark'; denial and deception (D&D); 'absence of the expected, presence of the unexpected'

By defining the baseline first, the system can surface *change* rather than *content* — it doesn't push everything, it pushes deltas against an established normal. This is how a system that perceives vastly more than the human filters to the human: it has internalized 'what usually happens here,' so it only escalates the departure. Negative indicators (expected-and-missing) are explicitly catalogued, because surprise often hides in absence, which naive alerting (which only fires on presence of activity) will never catch.

**Failure mode:** Baselines drift and get silently re-normalized, so a slowly rising threat is absorbed into 'the new normal' and never trips a delta (the gradualism problem again). Rich, noisy environments generate constant benign anomalies, flooding the human with deltas that don't matter (false-positive deluge). And a clever adversary masks intent inside normal-looking patterns (denial & deception, cover under routine exercises — exactly how 1973 and Crimea 2014 partly worked).

## Structured warning language and calibrated probability: standardized estimative verbs, words-of-estimative-probability (WEP, from Sherman Kent), confidence-vs-likelihood separation, and the explicit 'so what' / Bottom Line Up Front (BLUF). Warning products are written to a controlled vocabulary so that 'likely' means a defined probability band and is not misread, and the implication for the decision-maker leads rather than trails.

**Term of art:** Words of Estimative Probability (WEP); Sherman Kent; BLUF / 'so what'; confidence vs. likelihood; the Iraq WMD NIE failure; analytic calibration

The form in which something rises is standardized so the scarce human doesn't burn judgment decoding ambiguity. Kent's WEP was a direct response to the discovery that readers wildly mis-mapped words like 'probable' to numbers. BLUF/'so what' ensures the human gets the actionable conclusion and its consequence first — what to do / decide — not a chronological build-up. Confidence and likelihood are split so a high-stakes-but-uncertain warning can still be transmitted honestly without being dismissed as 'just a maybe.'

**Failure mode:** Estimative language is still chronically gamed for self-protection (vague hedging to be un-falsifiable), readers ignore the defined bands and re-impose their own priors, and 'high confidence' has been catastrophically wrong (Iraq WMD 2002 NIE — high-confidence judgments on absent weapons), teaching that calibrated-sounding language can launder bad sourcing. Over-standardized formats can also strip the texture that would let a sharp reader sense the weak, not-yet-nameable signal.

## Institutionalized contrarian and devil's-advocate machinery: Red Teams, Team A/Team B, the CIA's 'Red Cell,' Israeli military intelligence's mandated 'Devil's Advocate' office (Ipcha Mistabra, established after the 1973 surprise), and structured analytic techniques like Analysis of Competing Hypotheses (ACH) and key-assumptions checks.

**Term of art:** Ipcha Mistabra ('the opposite is probable' / Devil's Advocate office); Red Cell; Team A/Team B; Analysis of Competing Hypotheses (ACH); key-assumptions check; mitigating the 'conception'

These are standing mechanisms to force a competing interpretation to ALSO rise to the decision-maker, defeating the single dominant narrative ('conception') that suppresses disconfirming signals. Ipcha Mistabra ('the opposite is probable') is literally an office whose job is to write the dissent that contradicts the consensus warning picture and send it up the chain — guaranteeing the human sees the alternative reading, not just the comfortable one. ACH structurally requires analysts to seek evidence that DISCONFIRMS the leading hypothesis, countering confirmation bias at the level of method.

**Failure mode:** Devil's advocacy can become ritualized box-checking that everyone discounts ('that's just the red team being contrarian'), it can be marginalized organizationally, and it adds yet another voice to an already overloaded decision-maker (more signal competing for the same scarce attention). It also doesn't solve the action gap: the dissent rose before 1973 in various forms and was still overridden by the dominant conception.

## Decision-time / lead-time as the governing metric, and 'warning of warning' (warning that the situation is *trending* toward a threshold before it crosses). The field measures itself not by accuracy but by how much actionable lead time it buys the decision-maker, and it explicitly issues early, lower-confidence 'something is developing' alerts to put the decision-maker on notice and protect their option space.

**Term of art:** Warning lead time / decision time; 'warning of warning'; the warning paradox (certainty vs. timeliness trade-off); option space / freedom of action

What rises is timed to maximize the human's remaining freedom of action — a warning that arrives after options have closed is worthless even if perfectly accurate. 'Warning of warning' is a graduated, two-stage push: first a soft 'lean forward, this could become a problem' (preserving attention budget but priming the human), then the hard threshold alert. This protects the decision-maker's ability to *jointly* act with the system while there is still a decision to make, rather than presenting a fait accompli.

**Failure mode:** Early/soft warnings are the ones most easily dismissed ('you cried wolf last month'), and there's an inherent tension: the earlier you warn, the less certain you are, so the more deniable and ignorable the warning is (the warning paradox — maximum actionability and maximum certainty are inversely related and never coincide). Decision-makers also routinely consume the lead time with deliberation and still fail to act in the window the warner bought them.

## What this field knows
I&W's core insight is that warning is not the same as collection or analysis — it is a *pre-commitment discipline*. Long before any flood of signals arrives, analysts negotiate and write down, in advance, the small finite set of observable changes that would force a decision-maker's hand, and pre-assign meaning to each. The flood is then read against a fixed grammar of tripwires rather than re-interpreted from scratch every day. This field's deepest, hardest-won knowledge is about the *failure* of attention, not its success: it has institutionalized the study of surprise (Pearl Harbor, Yom Kippur, the Cuban Missile Crisis, 9/11) and built doctrine specifically to defeat the cognitive traps — normalcy bias, the 'cry wolf' habituation of repeated alerts, the signal-to-noise problem Roberta Wohlstetter named, and the tendency to explain away anomalies. Crucially, I&W separates the *act of warning* from the *act of deciding*: the warner's job is to ensure the decision-maker is never surprised and always has decision time, NOT to make the decision or even to be 'right' about whether the threat materializes. It treats a warning that prompts a defensive action later judged unnecessary as a SUCCESS, not a false alarm — inverting the normal cost function around attention. The interface lesson: what rises is governed by a jointly-authored, standing contract (the watch list / indicator list) that encodes meaning and required response ahead of time, paired with explicit thresholds, explicit ownership of the alert, and a forcing function that compels the human to acknowledge and act rather than merely observe.
